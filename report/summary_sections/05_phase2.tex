\section{Phase-2 direction: multi-node communication-efficient localization}

The thesis objective is now fixed as obtaining the best fleet localization accuracy with the least UWB communication effort. Two assumptions are treated as given: every node starts from a known initial pose, and every node carries both an IMU and a UWB transceiver and may participate in pairwise ranging.

The primary benchmark will use four symmetric mobile nodes, giving six possible UWB links. Three- and five-node fleets will be retained only as sensitivity cases. Unlike the earlier debugging simulations, ground-truth motion will be defined first through smooth global paths. Ideal velocity, acceleration, yaw, yaw rate, and body-frame IMU signals will then be derived from those paths before sensor noise and bias are added. Pairwise UWB measurements take the form
\begin{equation}
    z_{ij,k}=\|\bm p_{i,k}-\bm p_{j,k}\|_2+\nu_{ij,k},
\end{equation}
so both endpoints are uncertain mobile states.

The new Phase-2 sequence is:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{multi-node/full-communication benchmark:} four smooth mobile trajectories, known initial poses, independent IMUs, all six pairwise links available, IMU-only and all-pairs/\SI{10}{Hz} reference cases;
    \item \textbf{uniform rate sweep:} reduce all-pairs UWB rate to establish the basic localization--communication frontier;
    \item \textbf{fixed-budget link selection:} compare cyclic, random, geometry-aware, and information-aware peer/link choices at equal numbers of UWB exchanges and identical per-tick capacities;
    \item \textbf{reliability/outage stress:} packet loss, burst outages, and NLOS/outlier processes with an explicit measurement-reliability treatment where required;
    \item \textbf{IMU-quality sensitivity:} determine how the necessary communication budget changes with inertial noise and bias;
    \item \textbf{consolidated frontier:} fleet and worst-node RMSE versus normalized UWB exchanges, later extended to measured energy.
\end{enumerate}

Because the initial pose is known and the new problem is predominantly local/unimodal, a joint fleet EKF is the preferred primary estimator candidate. A short full-communication sanity comparison to the existing PF can be retained, but broad-prior global particle acquisition and AACOPF are no longer the main thesis path.

For \(N\) nodes define the communication ratio
\begin{equation}
    \rho_{\mathrm{comm}}
    =
    \frac{\sum_{k,i<j}a_{ij,k}}
         {K\binom{N}{2}},
\end{equation}
and use fleet position RMSE together with worst-node RMSE as the primary localization metrics. The central Phase-2 result should therefore be an empirical Pareto frontier between localization quality and communication effort.

Phase~3 will use this frontier to develop adaptive policies for \emph{when} to range and \emph{which pair} to range, using uncertainty, geometry/information gain, measurement reliability, and eventually measured energy cost.


\subsection{P2A benchmark result}
The four-node trajectory-first benchmark is now implemented. Across 20 matched seeds, IMU-only propagation gives mean fleet position RMSE \(2.322\pm0.605\)~m, while the maximum-communication reference using all six pairwise UWB links at \SI{10}{Hz} gives \(1.058\pm0.451\)~m. Full communication improves fleet RMSE in all 20 seeds.

The infrastructure-free error decomposition is more important than the aggregate reduction. The fleet-centroid RMSE is essentially unchanged (\SI{1.057}{m} versus \SI{1.056}{m}), while the centered relative-shape RMSE drops from \SI{2.027}{m} to \SI{0.046}{m}. Pairwise UWB therefore removes almost all differential fleet deformation but cannot directly observe a common translation of the complete fleet.

\subsection{P2B uniform-rate result}
On the frozen P2A benchmark, the all-six-link rate was swept across \(0,0.5,1,2,5,10\)~Hz with identical sensor draws in each of the 20 matched seeds. These rates cost respectively 0, 180, 360, 720, 1800, and 3600 individual pairwise range exchanges over \SI{60}{s}. Both endpoint conditions reproduce the P2A per-seed results.

Already at \SI{0.5}{Hz}, 180 exchanges (5\% of full communication) lower mean fleet RMSE from the IMU-only \SI{2.322}{m} to \SI{1.064}{m}; the \SI{10}{Hz} reference gives \SI{1.058}{m}. All positive rates improve fleet RMSE against IMU only in all 20 paired runs. This apparent plateau reflects the unobservable common translation: centroid RMSE remains near \SI{1.056}{m}, while relative-shape RMSE continues falling from \SI{0.114}{m} at \SI{0.5}{Hz} to \SI{0.046}{m} at \SI{10}{Hz}. Worst-node RMSE also improves from \SI{1.097}{m} to \SI{1.071}{m} over these rates.

\subsection{P2C matched-budget link selection}
P2C compared cyclic rotation, random choice, rolling geometric diversity, and EKF predicted relative-information gain at 180, 360, and 720 exchanges on the same 20 seeds. All four policies used identical exchange counts \emph{at every 10 Hz opportunity}, isolating pair choice within P2C. P2B's all-six-link rounds have different timing and serve only as an equal-count contextual reference.

At 360 exchanges, the information policy obtained \SI{0.0789}{m} mean relative-shape RMSE versus \SI{0.0824}{m} for cyclic and \SI{0.0914}{m} for random. The paired information-minus-cyclic difference was \(-0.00356\pm0.01174\)~m across 20 seeds (13 wins), and a descriptive 95\% paired-bootstrap interval crossed zero. Against random, the difference was \(-0.01250\pm0.01677\)~m (17 wins), with interval \([-0.01953,-0.00517]\)~m. At 180 exchanges the gains over cyclic were small and variable; at 720 the policy errors largely converged. Geometry was not consistently better than cyclic. The fleet centroid remained near \SI{1.056}{m}, so absolute fleet RMSE changed much less than relative-shape error.

The practical P2C decision is to retain cyclic as a strong low-complexity baseline and the information selector as a promising, budget-dependent comparator, without claiming a robust improvement over cyclic. P2D below tests missing and corrupted ranges at equal attempts.

After the infrastructure-free Phase-2 frontier is established, a separate P2G extension will test the value of a global reference. One of the existing four mobile nodes will first supply its true position throughout a matched run, followed by noisy and intermittent position reports. Comparisons will score the same three non-reference nodes and retain matched UWB budgets. This bounds how much a globally localized auxiliary can reduce common drift without changing the symmetric, unanchored main benchmark.

\subsection{P2D reliability at 360 attempted exchanges}
P2D ran 660 simulations: 20 matched seeds, cyclic and information schedules at identical times, and the differently timed P2B all-link 1 Hz reference. Seven separate reliability cases and a fixed NIS \(\leq9\) gate in four predefined cases were evaluated. Failures and rejected ranges consumed budget; attempted, received, and accepted counts remained distinct. Clean ordinary runs reproduced every P2B/P2C endpoint.

Cyclic shape RMSE rose from 0.0824 m to 0.0919 m and 0.1100 m under 30\% and 50\% loss. A network outage from 20 to 30 s gave 0.0917 m, with late shape accuracy close to clean. Information's advantage under missing data was small and uncertain. Under node-1 outage, repeated choices of its unavailable links left information with only 305.1 received ranges versus cyclic's 330.

Five percent positive outliers of 1--3 m raised ordinary cyclic shape RMSE to 0.2955 m. Gating reduced it to 0.0838 m, accepting 342.8 ranges and improving every seed; gated information gave 0.0826 m. Rejection mattered much more than specialized pair selection.

Persistent +0.75 m bias on link 1--2 from 20 to 40 s exposed a scheduling interaction. Ordinary information improved on ordinary cyclic (0.1478 versus 0.1690 m), but gated information was worse than gated cyclic (0.1305 versus 0.0956 m). Their paired gated difference was +0.03484 m, descriptive bootstrap 95\% interval [0.01997, 0.04804] m. Gated information spent about 49.8\% of attempts during the bias interval on that link, versus cyclic's 16.7\%, and its error remained elevated after the bias ended.

Retain cyclic as the practical reference and proceed to P2E IMU sensitivity. Reliability-aware selection is a separate future comparison against gated cyclic at equal attempts. These results cover one budget, fixed paths, stylized stresses, and a fixed gate; they do not validate hardware energy or estimator consistency.


\subsection{P2E IMU sensitivity}
P2E ran 900 simulations over the same 20 seeds and five cyclic budgets (0, 180, 360, 720, 3600). Six conditions varied white-noise and bias-drift amplitudes independently or together. The primary sweep froze process covariance; a companion rescaled its standard deviations with white noise for three conditions. All 100 reference endpoints reproduced earlier results. Sensors, initial-velocity errors, and Gaussian ranges used matched latent realizations. There was no gate or range failure.

At 360 exchanges, mean shape RMSE rose from reference 0.0824 m to 0.1569 m with fourfold white noise, 0.1562 m with tenfold bias drift, and 0.2070 m with both. At full communication their errors remained 0.1096, 0.1119, and 0.1527 m, versus reference 0.0456 m. Thus more ranges did not fully compensate for the tested sensor degradation. Centroid error remained dominant and almost insensitive to positive budgets within each sensor condition.

Halving white noise reduced fixed-process shape error by about 5.4 mm at 360 exchanges; halving the already small bias drift reduced it by only 0.9 mm. White-noise process rescaling helped some conditions, but did not remove the degraded-sensor floor. It did not model random-walk bias or establish estimator consistency.

A predefined 0.10 m mean shape target was reached by reference at 360 exchanges. Lower noise plus process rescaling reached it at 180 (0.0989 m), but only 11/20 seeds passed and its empirical 95th percentile was 0.1234 m. This is a descriptive threshold crossing, not a reliable 50\% communication saving. None of the degraded conditions reached the mean target even at full communication.

These fixed-path sensitivity conditions are not calibrated commercial-IMU grades, and the five-state EKF does not estimate bias. P2G remains the later optional global-reference comparison.


\subsection{P2F conditional accuracy--communication consolidation}
P2F adds no simulations. It audits 1960 source records from P2A--P2E, verifies 200 repeated endpoints, and retains 1760 distinct seed records across 88 designs. Attempted exchanges remain the cost proxy; failures and gate rejections consume budget. This is not measured radio energy.

Frontiers minimize attempted cost and error separately for each sensor, process-covariance, range-reliability, and update condition. Schedule and timing stay labelled: comparisons between uniform all-link rounds and P2C selection are whole-design comparisons, whereas cyclic versus information at matched P2C opportunities isolates pair choice. The empirical mean-shape frontier under clean reference settings chooses geometry at 180, information at 360, uniform at 720 and 1800, and full all-pairs at 3600; its ranking changes for worst-node error. Such empirical rankings do not establish robust policy superiority. Information's 360-attempt shape gain over cyclic is only 3.56 mm, with 13/20 improved seeds and a paired bootstrap interval including zero.

Cyclic at 360 attempts uses 10\% of full communication and meets the clean mean-shape target; 720 uses 20\% and meets the empirical p95 criterion. Pairwise ranging still leaves full-rate fleet RMSE near 1.058 m and centroid RMSE near 1.056 m. These results distinguish accurate relative geometry from globally accurate localization.

Reliability evidence remains a 360-attempt slice. Gating outliers reduces cyclic shape RMSE from 0.2955 to 0.0838 m, a larger effect than clean pair selection. Under persistent bias, gated information worsens cyclic by 0.03484 m, descriptive paired 95\% interval [0.01997, 0.04804] m. No tested reliability case meets the p95 target at this budget; no minimum reliability budget can be inferred from a single point. Percentiles describe seed-level trajectory RMSE, and bootstrap intervals are unadjusted descriptive summaries of these fixed paths.

P2A--P2F are complete. The next optional extension is P2G: one existing moving node supplies continuing global position information, with both conditions scored on the same three other nodes at matched UWB cost. Start with an ideal reference, then account for uncertainty and update costs in noisy or intermittent cases. Reliability-aware selection and bias-state estimation remain separate extensions.
