\chapter{Research Backlog}
\section{Phase 1 completion criteria}
Phase 1 is complete only after the following sequence is documented and reproducible.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{P1A -- IMU baseline:} deterministic planar IMU mechanization, noisy inertial drift, Gaussian UWB measurement, and conventional PF correction. Current status: \textbf{completed}. The implementation is verified and the result is supported by the P1B multi-seed study.
    \item \textbf{P1B -- robustness of the known-pose baseline:} multi-seed study; sensitivity to initial position/velocity/yaw uncertainty; particle count; IMU noise and bias levels; PF process-noise sensitivity. Current status: \textbf{completed and documented}. The known-pose PF improves position RMSE in all 20 baseline seeds, while large initialization errors and persistent IMU bias have been identified as explicit boundaries for later work.
    \item \textbf{P1C -- unknown initial pose:} introduce the global-localization problem using a first-range-conditioned position ring and globally distributed yaw hypotheses. Make the initial velocity prior explicit for the IMU-driven state. Current status: \textbf{completed as a diagnostic phase}. The known-yaw control is largely recoverable, whereas the realistic unknown-position/yaw bootstrap PF converges in only 2/20 core runs. Increasing particle count or disabling resampling does not resolve the failure. An idealized 40000-particle check with exact IMU/UWB information converges in 3/3 runs, showing that the selected moving-auxiliary scenario can resolve the ambiguity under ideal information.
    \item \textbf{P1D -- geometry/observability:} compare stationary, degenerate constant-bearing, and informative moving auxiliary trajectories; report convergence, particle-cloud behaviour, and finite-horizon observability singular values. Current status: \textbf{completed and documented}. The stationary single auxiliary has numerical rank four, the constant-bearing construction rank three, and only the informative moving auxiliary reaches full rank five. The two rank-deficient cases achieve no full-pose convergence in either realistic or idealized filtering, while the moving case is the only geometry with successful recovery. Realistic moving-case success is nevertheless only 5/20, showing that full local observability is necessary but not sufficient for robust bootstrap-PF global localization.
    \item \textbf{P1E -- paper reproduction audit:} document each equation and algorithmic step of Han et al.; list every underspecified AACOPF parameter and the chosen interpretation. Distinguish the paper's $\Delta L,\Delta\psi$ DR interface from the thesis IMU-driven mechanization. Current status: \textbf{completed and documented}. The audit identifies the main source ambiguities, including propagation timing, first-range sampling, the printed likelihood expression, $\alpha/\beta/\lambda$, the weight-difference definition, candidate selection, the undefined particle-rejection condition, adaptive-node thresholds, and the output ordering after uniform weight reset. A machine-readable interpretation contract fixes the P1F implementation choices.
    \item \textbf{P1F -- AACOPF implementation and evaluation:} implement the audited transition mechanism and compare conventional PF and AACOPF under matched conditions. Current status: \textbf{in progress; P1F-A, P1F-B, and P1F-C completed; P1F-D next}. P1F-A establishes the separate paper-state conventional-PF reference. P1F-B verifies the literal-small all-pairs ACO mechanism and exposes aggressive cloning and quadratic scaling. P1F-C performs a 60-setting safety-aware sweep and freezes $(\alpha,\beta,c_\lambda)=(0.5,0,2)$ after held-out validation achieves 40/40 correct-mode locks, 0/40 wrong locks, and 9/40 catastrophic ancestry-collapse events. The remaining sequence is P1F-D matched PF--AACOPF comparison in informative geometry, P1F-E rank-deficient negative controls, P1F-F non-Gaussian/outlier stress, and P1F-G scalable bounded-candidate adaptation.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Current Phase-1 parameter decisions after P1F-C}
For the local known-pose experiments, the P1B conclusion remains valid: $N_p=5000$ particles is a conservative default, and $N_p=2500$ is acceptable for exploratory sweeps. The P1A PF process-noise values are retained because P1B shows similar performance for the baseline and a $2\times$ scaling, whereas substantially smaller process noise causes under-dispersion and occasional failures.

The IMU-driven global-pose problem requires a different interpretation of particle count. A structured $100\times100$ position/yaw grid ($N_p=10000$) remains the practical global-search baseline for realistic matched diagnostic experiments. P1C showed that increasing this grid to $150\times150$ did not make convergence reliable. The idealized $200\times200$ ($N_p=40000$) configurations are therefore diagnostic tests of representability/identifiability rather than practical defaults.

P1D establishes that geometry must be treated before particle-algorithm tuning. The stationary single-auxiliary and constant-bearing cases are rank deficient and therefore serve as negative controls: no particle-transition strategy should be interpreted as solving the missing-information problem in those geometries. The informative moving case is full rank but remains difficult for the bootstrap PF; this is the appropriate principal case for testing whether AACOPF improves preservation and selection of competing global modes.

P1E fixes a second, separate paper-level baseline. Han et al. are reproduced first with the three-state $[x,y,\phi]$ model and higher-level $\Delta L,\Delta\phi$ inputs. The primary propagation convention follows their formal Equation~(3), while the Algorithm~1 post-turn convention is retained as a sensitivity variant. Random first-range annular initialization is used for the paper-level reproduction; the annulus width $\Delta d=3\sigma_{\mathrm{UWB}}$ is an explicit repository choice because the paper does not report $\Delta d$.

P1F-A fixes the practical conventional-PF comparison population at $N_p=10000$, with $N_p=40000$ retained as an upper-population reference. The 20-seed study shows that simply increasing particle count does not monotonically or reliably solve the global-mode problem. Correct-region hypotheses are present initially even in the failing runs, so the key difficulty is survival and selection of the useful mode under sequential likelihood weighting and particle replacement.

The primary AACOPF interpretation uses a Gaussian range likelihood, strictly higher-weight destination candidates, weight difference $w_j-w_i$, synchronous movement from the pre-transition cloud, pre-ACO posterior output, and a uniform post-transition weight reset. P1F-B initially evaluated $(\alpha,\beta,c_\lambda)=(1,1,0.5)$ as a mechanism-check point and showed that this configuration is too aggressive for reliable use: individual updates can replace nearly the complete cloud and eliminate the correct mode.

P1F-C now freezes the literal-small comparison tuple
\begin{equation}
    (\alpha,\beta,c_\lambda)=(0.5,0,2).
\end{equation}
The tuple is selected from 60 candidates using 16 matched development runs and then evaluated on 40 held-out runs. Held-out correct-mode lock is 40/40 and wrong-mode lock is 0/40. Catastrophic ancestry collapse still occurs in 9/40 runs and a dominant-clone event in 34/40 runs. Therefore this setting is accepted only as a frozen parameterization for P1F-D; it is not considered diversity-safe or deployment-ready. The fact that the three highest-ranked development settings all use $\beta=0$ shows that the inverse-distance term is unnecessary for safe mode selection in the present controlled bimodal experiment, but this observation is not generalized beyond that setting.

The computational distinction between literal reproduction and later deployment remains unchanged. Han et al. recommend $N=M=400$, corresponding to $160000$ particles. A literal dense interpretation contains $2.56\times10^{10}$ pair entries; one \texttt{float64} pair matrix alone requires approximately \SI{204.8}{GB}. P1F-B measures transition runtimes up to $N_p=1200$ and confirms rapid approximately quadratic growth. The literal implementation is therefore restricted to mechanism validation and manageable populations. Any bounded-candidate large-population version remains a scalable adaptation and must not be labelled as a literal reproduction.

Persistent accelerometer/gyroscope bias remains an open estimator-design question rather than an immediately added state. The P1B stress test shows that unestimated bias can dominate the remaining error, but the selected stress levels are synthetic. The decision to augment the state with bias variables should therefore be made after real IMU bias/noise characteristics are available.

The initial velocity assumption also remains explicit in the IMU-driven branch. P1C shows that free initial velocity substantially worsens the global search. The aligned fixed-speed prior is therefore retained through the five-state Phase-1 diagnostic studies so that the separate initial-velocity problem is not confounded with observability and particle management. The paper-level three-state P1F branch does not introduce velocity states because Han et al. absorb motion into $\Delta L$ and $\Delta\phi$.

\section{Immediate P1F sequence after P1F-C}
The next experiment is \textbf{P1F-D}. It will perform the matched conventional-PF versus AACOPF comparison in the informative moving geometry using common trajectories, range-noise realizations, initial particle clouds, and declared particle budgets. The AACOPF parameters must remain fixed at $(\alpha,\beta,c_\lambda)=(0.5,0,2)$ throughout the P1F-D evaluation. The comparison must retain correct-mode survival, convergence, unique-parent fraction, destination multiplicity, moved-particle fraction, pre-ACO effective sample size, and runtime alongside localization error.

P1F-E retains the stationary and constant-bearing rank-deficient geometries as negative controls. P1F-F adds outlier/non-Gaussian ranging. P1F-G then replaces the dense destination set by a bounded/scalable candidate mechanism and quantifies the accuracy--diversity--runtime trade-off before any embedded transfer.

\section{Paper-reproduction architecture after P1F-C}
The earlier Option-A/Option-B decision remains resolved in favor of \textbf{two baselines}. The paper-level reproduction uses $[x,y,\phi]$ with $\Delta L,\Delta\phi$ so that the AACOPF mechanism can be assessed without the additional initialization problem introduced by velocity states. The hardware-oriented thesis estimator remains $[p_x,p_y,v_x,v_y,\psi]$ with raw IMU mechanization. Only after the paper-level AACOPF and its scalable adaptation are understood will a selected particle-management concept be transferred to the IMU-driven estimator.

The P1E audit fixes the claim boundary. Exact numerical replication of Han et al. is not required because raw trajectories, ranging data, random seeds, update frequency, complete noise distributions, and several AACOPF tuning parameters are unavailable. Phase 1 instead requires a transparent qualitative reproduction of the problem structure, observability behavior, and audited particle-management mechanism under fully documented assumptions. P1F-C strengthens this boundary: its frozen parameters are explicitly repository-selected and must not be presented as values used by Han et al.

\section{Phase 2--4 outlook}
Phase 2 will vary UWB update rate, node count, geometry, outages, LOS/NLOS degradation, and IMU quality. P1D already establishes the principle that range count alone is not a sufficient measure of localization information; Phase 2 should therefore retain geometry descriptors when comparing communication rates and node-selection strategies. P1F-B/P1F-C add a second practical requirement: particle-management diversity and computational cost must be measured alongside localization accuracy because a method can achieve high posterior confidence by destructive mode collapse and because the literal AACOPF transition is computationally unsuitable at large populations. Phase 3 will develop uncertainty/information-aware UWB scheduling and node selection. Phase 4 will characterize the actual hardware, replace simulated sensor parameters with measured ones, measure energy per UWB transaction, port the selected estimator to C/C++, and compare simulation and hardware.
